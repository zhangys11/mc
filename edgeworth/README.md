## What the report claims

Finite-sample null failure is not one mechanism, and §3 separates three:

- **A — standardised means.** Genuine $O(n^\{-1/2\})$ Edgeworth term. The textbook coefficient $p\_1=-\\gamma\_1(x^2-1)/6$ applies to the standardised mean; for a non-linear $H$ the rate survives but the constant does not, and a symmetric parent does **not** imply an $O(n^\{-1\})$ error (§3.1 gives the counterexample).

- **B — quadratic forms and likelihood ratios.** The limit is $Q=\\tfrac12\\sum\_j\\lambda\_j\\chi^2\_\{1,j\}$; it is $\\chi^2$ exactly iff $\\tfrac12\\Sigma^\{1/2\}A\\Sigma^\{1/2\}$ is a projection. The $n^\{-1/2\}$ Edgeworth term cancels by parity here, so the error is $o(n^\{-1/2\})$ and an $O(n^\{-1\})$ rate needs the second-order condition stated in Proposition 5; what binds is the $O(1)$ shape of the limit law. A scalar correction can restore a $\\chi^2\_\\nu$ reference only when the weights are equal, which is why Bartlett's $B^\*$ works to first order and Box's $M$ has no scalar analogue for the parents studied (§3.3, §5.6).

- **C — discrete support.** A continuity term of order $n^\{-1/2\}$; for the symmetric binomial it is exactly $\\varphi(0)n^\{-1/2\}$ (§3.2, Proposition 3). For rank and $2\\times k$ table statistics no closed-form coefficient is claimed — only the empirical pattern of §7.1.

Study 1 (normal sampling) shows the predicted split — the tests above the Monte Carlo floor are the discrete-valued ones — and Study 2 varies the assumptions test by test, with each cell read according to whether a rejection is a size error or a correct rejection of a different $H\_0$.  The corrections are compared **paired on the same replications** at four nominal levels with McNemar intervals (§7.6, Table 6), the sign test is read against its exact attainable binomial levels (Table S7 and Figure 3B), power is reported raw and size-adjusted at a common actual size (Table 7), the KS resolution of the discrete tests is re-checked at $N=2\\times10^5$ (Table S8), and four resampling schemes for Box's $M$ are compared in five null cells (Table 8). Two results deserve emphasis: the raw power advantage of both corrections disappears (Bartlett) or vanishes entirely (sign test) once both statistics are evaluated at exactly 5%, and the $n^\**\{KS\}$* *column mixes a real population distance with simulation resolution.  For Box's $M$, §5.6 and* *Appendix A give the asymptotic weight decomposition of the null law (Table A1): all weights equal 1* *under normality, but $\{5,3,3\}$ under the shared-scale $t\_5$ parent and* *$\{56.47,56.47,1\}$ under the independent-coordinate lognormal parent ($p=2$), so no scalar* *correction can restore $\\chi^2*\\nu$ for those parents.

The multivariate case is the boundary: under a normal parent Box's $M$ has an exact matrix-Dirichlet null law with a closed-form CGF and critical values obtainable without simulation (§5.4–5.5); off normality the limit is a weighted sum whose weights are unequal for the parents studied, so no scalar correction exists for them (§5.6). The finite-sample failure of the plug-in $M^\*$ under normality is a separate phenomenon — estimator bias, ratio variability and clipping, not a shape mismatch (§7.5, Table 5).

## Known gaps

- The multi-level comparison covers the two corrected tests only; the other tests are read at $\\alpha=0.05$. For the sign test the attainable sizes are given exactly (Table S7 and Figure 3B).

- The resampling comparison is finite: five null cells, $N=1\{,\}000$ outer replications, $B=200$ resamples. It establishes directions, not practical calibration.

- The weight decomposition of Table A1 is asymptotic (equal group sizes, common spherical covariance); it is not a finite-$n$ expansion.

- The KS resolution study stops at $N=2\\times10^5$; no $10^6$ reference is computed.

- The sample-size index is not uniform across tests: T4 uses a total of $\\max(5n,30)$ while the others use a per-group size; Figure 2 and Table S4 say so.

- Four resampling schemes are evaluated (parametric, within-group, null-imposed, permutation). The null-imposed scheme is the right target but is not perfect at $n=30$; permutation fails in the equal-covariance, different-distribution cells because exchangeability does not hold there.
